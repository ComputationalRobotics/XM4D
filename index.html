Applications

XM4D’s motion is explicit: a graph of nodes, each with a position and a rotation per frame, that carries the dynamic Gaussians. The reconstruction can therefore be edited and re-animated. A motion can drive a robot, a node can carry a known object’s 6D pose, or a generated mesh’s next to the hands that hold it, parts can be posed by hand, and the moving object can be left out.

Dance with a robot

For people, the graph’s nodes are the joints of a body pose, so the solved motion is a skeleton animation. A Unitree G1 humanoid follows it through inverse kinematics on its own joints.

Open and close each door yourself

A kitchen’s digital twin, from a robot opening it

In a RoboCasa kitchen, a mobile robot opens and closes seven parts in turn, while the camera follows it: a drawer, two wall cabinets, the dishwasher, both fridge doors and the microwave. Each part is one rigid node. From its solved poses, XM4D tells a hinge from a slide and fits its axis; all seven get the right kind of joint. In the 3D viewer, drag a part’s slider to open it, and the view goes to it.

How it works

Lift tracks with predicted depth

Point trackers and a monocular depth model give 2D tracks and per-frame depth. Every tracked point is lifted to 3D, and the tracks are split into static and dynamic ones.

Certified cameras

A scaled bundle adjustment over camera poses, per-frame depth scales, and static landmarks reduces to a QCQP over scaled rotations. Its SDP relaxation is solved by a GPU Riemannian staircase that returns a certificate of global optimality.

Certified non-rigid motion

Dynamic motion is an embedded deformation graph whose node transformations are expressed relative to one rest frame. Tracking, rigidity, and smoothness then reduce to a rotation-only QCQP that the same certified solver handles.

4D Gaussian splatting

The certified cameras and graph motion initialize dynamic Gaussian splatting, which refines appearance and geometry for novel-view rendering.

This work · journal extension

XM4D: what is new

  • A static solver carried over from XM that solves bundle adjustment to certified global optimality in a few minutes.
  • A dynamic stage: an embedded deformation graph whose tracking, rigidity, and temporal smoothness terms form a rotation-only QCQP, solved by the same certifiable solver.
  • The certified cameras and motion initialize dynamic Gaussian splatting. Across seven benchmarks: leading camera accuracy, fast and scalable optimization, and the best average novel-view rendering on DyCheck, NVIDIA, and Syn4D.

Abstract

Accurate 4D reconstruction remains challenging: jointly estimating camera motion, scene geometry, and non-rigid deformation entails highly nonconvex optimization, making global optimality difficult to guarantee. We present XM4D, a framework for accurate and efficient 4D reconstruction built upon certifiably globally optimal geometric optimization. By separating the estimation of camera motion from non-rigid deformation, we formulate both stages as structured quadratically constrained quadratic programs (QCQP) that admit semidefinite programming (SDP) relaxations and verifiable global optimality certificates. For camera motion estimation, we lift static point tracks into 3D using foundational depth prediction models and formulate a scaled bundle adjustment problem that jointly estimates camera poses, depth scales, and static landmarks. By analytically eliminating translations and landmarks, we reduce the problem to a QCQP over scaled rotations. For non-rigid reconstruction, we represent dynamic motion using an embedded deformation graph (EDG) and show that its tracking, rigidity, and temporal regularization objectives similarly reduce to a rotation-only QCQP. Both problems admit SDP relaxations that we solve efficiently with a GPU-accelerated Burer–Monteiro solver based on Riemannian optimization. The recovered geometry further initializes dynamic Gaussian splatting for photometric refinement. Extensive experiments across seven benchmarks demonstrate accurate camera motion estimation on both static and dynamic scenes, efficient optimization that scales to thousands of frames, and state-of-the-art novel-view rendering quality.
